Research & Projects

Bachelor’s thesis · 2026 · Saarland University / CISPA

Robustness of AI-Generated Image Detection Against Localized Inpainting Attacks

Evaluated six detection methods across three approaches to investigate how localized image edits affect AI-generated image detection. The work covered experimental design, model inference, statistical evaluation and interpretation of detector behavior.

  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • pandas
  • NumPy
  • scikit-learn
Thesis & source code ↗
Localized image manipulation

A localized edit changes the hat band while retaining the surrounding photograph. This is one of the semantic manipulation examples examined in the thesis.

Passive classifiersUFD · DIMD
Training-free methodsAEROBLADE · WaRPAD
Proactive watermarkingTree-Ring · Stable Signature
Research findings

Robustness depended on the image source, edit type and detection approach. Strong performance on unedited images did not necessarily transfer to localized edits.

Experimental design

Baseline calibration & attack scenarios

Established baselines with 200 real and 200 synthetic images per baseline evaluation. Kept decision thresholds fixed during attack testing and compared localized edits produced with LaMa and ZITS, including different mask sizes and shapes.

Model evaluation

Metrics & statistical analysis

Used Python and PyTorch for inference and pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn for analysis. Compared ROC-AUC, accuracy and attack success rates, with confidence intervals to quantify uncertainty.

Research outputs

Reproducibility & interpretation

Published the code and thesis on GitHub and curated datasets on Hugging Face. Analyzed differences between edited real photographs and modified synthetic images, including the behavior of watermark-based methods.

Papers

What is Augmented Reality?↗

March 2021

An examination of what distinguishes augmented reality from other digital technologies, how its scene generation, tracking and display components work, and where it can be applied. The paper considers uses in education, medicine, engineering, manufacturing and entertainment, alongside questions of social acceptance. It also discusses technical limitations, including display constraints, delays and registration errors, and argues that AR is likely to become an enduring part of everyday life.

Where Our Society Is Heading, Utopia or Dystopia?↗

April 2021

A critical discussion of whether transhumanism could move society toward a utopian or dystopian future. The paper examines uncertain long-term consequences, dependence on embedded technologies, unequal access to enhancement and pressure on individuals to remain competitive. Drawing on ethical, social and moral arguments, it concludes that these risks could deepen inequality and undermine autonomy unless enhancement technologies are carefully tested and controlled.

Embracing Human Enhancement from Different Aspects↗

March 2021

An analysis of human enhancement from individual, social, political and economic perspectives. The paper weighs potential improvements in physical and cognitive capabilities against concerns about fairness, access, changing definitions of human nature and commercial transparency. It argues in favor of embracing enhancement for its potential benefits, while recognizing that unequal availability and the marketing of these technologies raise questions that require attention.

Adverse Effects of Mass Tourism↗

December 2021

An assessment of whether the economic benefits of mass tourism outweigh its costs to host communities. The paper examines the commercialization of local culture, environmental degradation, pressure on natural resources and economic leakage. It also considers the argument that tourism creates employment, while questioning the consequences of dependence on wage labor and tourism income. Its conclusion is that these cultural, environmental and economic harms outweigh the benefits examined.

Towards Being An Ethical Tourist↗

November 2021

An exploration of how travelers can make more responsible choices, comparing perspectives from tourists and host communities in the cited literature. The paper considers environmental impacts, wildlife treatment, respect for local customs and support for local businesses. It challenges the claim that ethical tourism is impossible and argues that informed decisions, cultural awareness and attention to local economic wellbeing can make travel more considerate of its hosts.
